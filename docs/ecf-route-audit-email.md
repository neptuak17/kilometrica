# The route audit, for ECF

Text for an email to the European Cyclists' Federation (ECF) on how the route audit works and how it lines up with the EuroVelo European Certification Standard (ECS). Written 2026-11-03; the figures come from the EV8 and EV15 audits of that day.

## Email text

Kilometrica's route audit was built to give riders planning a EuroVelo trip an honest picture of every stretch, and it follows the structure of the European Certification Standard. It starts from the route's official GPX and development status, as published on EuroVelo.com under the ODbL, matches the track to OpenStreetMap, and measures everything along it: the kind of way and the traffic on it, the surface, the gradients (from the Copernicus elevation model), and the places to stay, eat, get a bike repaired, find a toilet or take a train or ferry with a bike. Each fact is placed at its kilometre with its source and date. As in an ECS assessment, the route is divided into 1 km minor sections within EuroVelo's own daily sections. Each criterion is marked pass, fail or unknown for every kilometre, and the results are added up for each daily section and for the whole route as the share of length that meets all the essential criteria and all the important ones, which is how ECS certification reports summarise a route. Where the map data are too thin to judge a kilometre, it is marked unknown rather than guessed. So far we have audited all of EuroVelo 8 and EuroVelo 15 along one bank of the Rhine, and the same audit runs on any route supplied as a GPX file.

The criteria are grouped in the ECS tiers. The essential ones are continuity (no missing links, and the route follows mapped ways), traffic safety (no more than 100 m of heavy or fast traffic in any kilometre), accommodation, food and water, and cycle repair on every daily section, and a station that takes bikes at least every 150 km. The important ones are low traffic, a paved or compacted surface, gradients (no 100 m stretch steeper than 8%), signposting (from EuroVelo's published status), accommodation other than camping and a bike shop on every daily section, toilets every 15 km, and a station that takes bikes every 75 km. The services criteria follow the wording of the 2026 ECS short manual, including its limit of 5 km, or 30 minutes in mountain areas, for accommodation to count. Alongside the criteria, the audit lists findings at their kilometre: busy roads by name, unpaved stretches, steps, steep climbs, long stretches with no station to bail out to, and each gap typed by the evidence for it (a ferry, an official public-transport link, or a missing link). It also reports data-quality findings that may be useful to ECF and the NECCs: straight-line placeholders in the GPX (one of 28 km on EuroVelo 8), breaks between sections, a section stored end to start, ridden lengths more than 10% off the published length, and daily sections where OpenStreetMap's EuroVelo route relation is missing or incomplete.

We see the audit as an indicative pre-assessment that sits alongside ECS certification, not in place of it. The infrastructure thresholds are provisional, kept in a plain rules file so they can be set to the manual's own values; traffic is estimated from OpenStreetMap's road classes, speed limits and lanes rather than measured counts; and a few criteria aren't checked yet, among them how often public transport runs, path width, the signs on the ground and the promotion criteria. As a first check, EuroVelo 15, more than half of which is certified, meets all the essential criteria on 93% of its length in our audit, against 56% for EuroVelo 8, in line with how far each route has been developed. Our next step is to calibrate the audit against a published certification report, starting with the 2025 report for EuroVelo 15, and we would value ECF's view on the thresholds and on which findings would help you and the National EuroVelo Coordination Centres most.

## Behind the text

- **Criteria and their wording:** `engine/profiles/eurovelo-ecs-v0.yaml`; the accommodation limit is in `engine/profiles/services-v0.yaml`. Decisions D13 (placeholder thresholds), D17 (access limits), D20 (stations without timetables) and D21 (the 2026 short manual's services wording).
- **Figures:** route-level `scores.json` in the bundles of 2026-11-03: EV8 meets the essential criteria on 55.6% of 7,213 km (its main line, section 152 set aside), EV15 on 93.0% of 1,463 km (the main line, D65). Re-check them if the email goes out after another audit.
- **Before sending (D5):** nothing in the email should suggest ECF endorses Kilometrica; EuroVelo data is credited under the ODbL.
- **If ECF asks for results:** the busy-road rule may count rural mountain roads as busy (section 13), which lowers traffic safety and low traffic. EV15's certified sections count as signed since D105 (their GPX status reads "Certified" with no word on signs); before, its important-criteria share was 14%, now 57%.
